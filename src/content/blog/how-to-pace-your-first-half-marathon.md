Most first-time half marathoners do not run out of fitness. They run out of patience in the first 5 km. Here is a simple way to plan your race so the last 6 km feel strong instead of survival.

## 1. Pick a goal pace you have already run

Your goal pace should be a pace you have held in training, not a number you hope for. A reliable check: run a 10 km time trial or a recent 10 km race, then add roughly 15–25 seconds per kilometre for your half marathon pace.

If your last 10 km was 50:00 (5:00/km), a realistic half marathon pace is around 5:15–5:25/km — a finish time of about 1:51–1:54.

## 2. Split the race into thirds

- **km 0–7:** goal pace **plus** 5–10 seconds per km. It should feel almost too easy.
- **km 7–15:** settle into exact goal pace. This is where the race is won.
- **km 15–21.1:** run on effort. If you have anything left, this is where you spend it.

Starting 10 seconds per km too fast costs you far more than 10 seconds per km at the end — the cost compounds.

## 3. Practise the pace before race day

Two workouts are enough:

1. **Long run with a goal-pace block** — 16–18 km easy, with the final 6 km at goal pace.
2. **Tempo intervals** — 3 × 3 km at goal pace with 3 minutes of easy jogging between.

If you cannot complete the second workout at your intended pace, your goal is too aggressive. Adjust it now, not at km 16.

## 4. Fuel earlier than you think

Take your first gel at around 40 minutes, then every 30–40 minutes after that. Drink small amounts at every station rather than a lot at one. Test this in training — race day is not the time to find out how your stomach reacts.

## 5. Have a plan B

Heat, wind, and a bad night's sleep are all real. Decide in advance what your plan B pace is (usually goal pace plus 10–15 seconds per km) so you make that decision calmly at km 5 rather than falling apart at km 16.
