21.1 km is the distance where preparation stops being optional. You can improvise a 10K. A half marathon exposes every gap in your endurance, fuelling and pacing.

## Where you should start

Comfortably running 8–10 km and covering 20–25 km a week is the right launch point. If you are below that, spend a month building easy mileage before starting the plan below.

## What the training has to build

- **Aerobic endurance** — from the weekly long run.
- **Sustained speed** — from tempo running at close to half marathon effort.
- **Efficiency** — from strides, intervals and strength work.
- **Fuelling practice** — most runners need 30–60 g of carbohydrate per hour beyond 75 minutes.

## The 12-week plan (4 runs per week)

| Week | Tempo / interval | Long run | Weekly total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 20 min tempo | 10 km | ~28 km |
| 2 | 5 × 800 m at 5K pace | 12 km | ~31 km |
| 3 | 2 × 12 min tempo | 14 km | ~34 km |
| 4 | 25 min tempo | 10 km (easier) | ~28 km |
| 5 | 4 × 1 km at 10K pace | 15 km | ~37 km |
| 6 | 30 min tempo | 16 km | ~40 km |
| 7 | 3 × 2 km at half pace | 12 km (easier) | ~34 km |
| 8 | 2 × 15 min tempo | 18 km | ~43 km |
| 9 | 6 × 1 km at 10K pace | 19 km | ~45 km |
| 10 | 40 min tempo | 16 km with last 5 km at goal pace | ~42 km |
| 11 | 20 min tempo | 12 km | ~30 km |
| 12 | 4 × 400 m at 5K pace | Race day | ~20 km |

Keep every other run genuinely easy. The plan only works if the easy days are easy.

## Long run rules

- Run it 60–90 seconds per km slower than goal race pace.
- Build the longest run to 18–19 km. You do not need to run 21 km in training; race-day adrenaline covers the difference.
- Practise your race breakfast, race shoes, and your gels on the long run — never for the first time on race day.

## Fuelling and hydration

Beyond 75 minutes, take one gel every 35–45 minutes with water. Drink to thirst; in hot, humid conditions plan 400–700 ml per hour and add electrolytes. Test this in weeks 8–10 so nothing on race day is new.

## The taper

Reduce volume by 25% in week 11 and 40–50% in week 12 while keeping short, sharp intensity. Tapering feels wrong — heavy legs, restlessness, phantom niggles — and it still works. Fitness is built weeks earlier; the taper simply lets it surface.

## Race-day pacing for 21.1 km

Run the first 5 km 5–10 seconds per km slower than goal pace, settle into goal pace from 5 to 15 km, then decide at 15 km whether you have anything left. Runners who negative-split their first half marathon almost always beat runners who went out fast, even with identical fitness.

## Common first-timer mistakes

1. Racing every training run.
2. Skipping strength work, then developing calf or knee pain in week 8.
3. Adding long-run distance more than 2 km per week.
4. Chasing a goal time set by someone else's finish line photo.
